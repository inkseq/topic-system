## 1. Header

**Logline:** When the mess-up can't reach his drink and calls Aesop's crow a fairy tale, the explainer shows the stone trick works - and that real rooks and crows pass the same test.

**Takeaway:** Dropping stones to raise water isn't just a fable: rooks and crows solve it in experiments, and they pick stones over sand.

**Core / casual / new:** core 4 - fable turned true is our 'wrong first' sweet spot; casual 4 - everyone knows the fable; new 4 - bird + glass + stones reads instantly

- *reaction* - A crow can solve this. Can you? / "CROW 1, HUMAN 0"
- *object* - The bird trick from a 2,500-year-old story / "STONES RAISE WATER"
- *contrast* - Sand vs stones: which raises the water? / "SAND OR STONES?"

**Wants this episode:**

- MESS-UP: to finish his drink without listening to a moral
- EXPLAINER: to let the glass prove the point
- ASKER: to know if the old story is true

## 6. Dialogue script (for the voice pass)

```

INT. WORKSHOP KITCHEN - AFTERNOON

           EXPLAINER
              (dropping a pebble into a glass)
          A crow can solve this. You can't. Yet.

             MESS-UP
              (sucking air)
          That's a fable. Birds aren't that clever. Prove it.

               ASKER
              (opening notebook)
          Wait. The crow and the pitcher? That really happens?

             MESS-UP
          No. It's a bedtime story. Moral, nap, done.

           EXPLAINER
          Partly. The moral is made up. The trick isn't.

[Illustrated page of the old fable: a crow dropping stones into a pitcher]

               ASKER
          So what's the trick? Drop stones until the water rises?

             MESS-UP
              (grabbing a sponge)
          Easy. I'll just soak it up with this.

               ASKER
          The sponge drank it. Now there's even less juice.

           EXPLAINER
          You picked the one thing that makes it worse.


INT. WORKSHOP - DEMO BENCH

[Two tubes of water on a bench: one with sand ready, one with stones ready]

           EXPLAINER
          Stones push water up. Sand mostly just soaks it in.

             MESS-UP
              (confident)
          Sand is smaller, so it works better. Obviously.

             MESS-UP
              (tapping the tube)
          Why isn't it moving? It's tiny! Tiny should be better!

               ASKER
          It barely moved. The stones one is already rising.

             MESS-UP
              (sulking)
          Fine. But a real bird wouldn't know that.

           EXPLAINER
          In 2009, scientists asked some rooks exactly that.

           EXPLAINER
          Each rook got a tube, a floating worm, and stones.

           EXPLAINER
          The worm floated too low to reach. So they dropped stones.

               ASKER
              (writing fast)
          They used stones until they could reach the worm?

           EXPLAINER
          And they picked big stones over small ones.

             MESS-UP
              (whispering)
          Smarter than me with the sand.

               ASKER
              (grinning)
          Write that down: a bird beat you at physics.

               ASKER
          Do crows do it too, or just rooks?

           EXPLAINER
          Crows do too. Researchers watched them pass the same test.

             MESS-UP
              (grabbing pebbles)
          Right. My turn. Pebbles. Big ones. Watch this.


INT. WORKSHOP KITCHEN - AFTERNOON

[Back at the tall glass: mess-up drops pebbles one by one; the juice climbs]

             MESS-UP
              (triumphant)
          It's rising! I'm basically a crow now!

           EXPLAINER
          Partly. The crow didn't overfill the glass.

[The glass overflows onto the table]

               ASKER
          So the fable was right about the trick, not the bird?

           EXPLAINER
          It was right about both. Some birds really do this.

             MESS-UP
          Then why did I think fables were all made up?

           EXPLAINER
          Some morals are made up. Some observations are older than science.

               ASKER
              (writing)
          Things you were wrong about: crows, sand, and juice.

             MESS-UP
          Partly wrong.

```
